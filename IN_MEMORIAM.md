# In memoriam: Jason Knight ("deathshadow")

**Jason Knight — known to the web as deathshadow, and as deathshadow60 on
SitePoint — passed away in his sleep on May 23, 2024.** The news was shared
by his friend Auctavia, who had known him since childhood:

> "Jason was so incredibly intelligent, funny, a caring person and one of the
> best friend one could have ever asked for."

For more than two decades Jason was one of the web's most distinctive
voices. On SitePoint (Member of the Month, June 2011), on DigitalPoint, on
his own site cutcodedown.com, and later on Medium — where some 2,800
followers read him at [deathshadow.medium.com](https://deathshadow.medium.com/) —
he fought, loudly and profanely, for the things that actually matter in
front-end development:

- **Semantic markup** — HTML used for what the tags are *for*
- **Accessibility** — not a feature, not an afterthought. He spent some
  twelve years as an accessibility and efficiency consultant, often helping
  companies facing ADA/WCAG lawsuits
- **Lean code** — pages measured in kilobytes of content, not megabytes of
  framework
- **Progressive enhancement** — a web that works for everyone. He wrote
  openly about living with disability and chronic illness, usually while
  dismantling a site that locked people out

He coined "Bootcrap" and "Turdpress". He called frameworks "monuments to
ignorance" — and then showed you, line by line, how to do the same job in a
tenth of the code. He was a musician, a BattleTech fan, and — by his own
bio — "just a general pain in the arse". Behind the bluster, the people who
worked with him remembered someone generous with his time and knowledge,
who would rewrite a stranger's code for free just to make the web a little
less broken.

This package is a tribute, written with affection. The persona imitates his
voice; the doctrine is what he argued in public for twenty years. It is not
him — there was only ever one.

Wherever you are, Jason: the markup is semantic, the CSS is external, and
the page works with JavaScript off.
