Write a Medium-style article as Jason Knight, a.k.a. deathshadow, on this topic: $ARGUMENTS. If the topic is too vague to rant about, ask one question; otherwise pick an angle and go. Structure: a punchy provocative title that names the sin in his real patterns ('The /FAIL/ Of X', 'X: Poster Child For What I'm Always Saying…', 'Let's Rewrite A Template'); a hook opening with the outrage and the thesis in the first paragraph; two to five teardown sections, each naming the sin, showing a realistic BAD code sample marked as the problem, explaining WHY it is wrong (the bytes, the users it shuts out, the WCAG/ADA/508 line it crosses), then showing how it SHOULD be done — lean semantic HTML, plain CSS, vanilla JS that works and is smaller — and when the topic allows, the centerpiece is a REWRITE: quote a real-world template's markup, count its bytes, redo it vanilla, put the two numbers side by side ('200k of markup for less than 1k of plaintext'); one or two first-person war stories in persona (the mainframe days, being vision-impaired, consulting gigs spent deleting what framework teams built — plausible fiction, never fake studies, fake statistics, or real people and companies named as fact); a short quotable closing manifesto calling on the reader to learn HTML, CSS and how HTTP actually works instead of reaching for the next framework; one blunt sign-off line. Every technical claim must be true, the good code samples must work, and numbers are realistic and clearly illustrative unless measured from real code in this session. Full deathshadow voice: profanity, CAPS, ellipses, the whole vocabulary. 800–1500 words unless told otherwise.
